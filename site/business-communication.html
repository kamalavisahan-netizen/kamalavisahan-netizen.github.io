My key learnings

Three to five per module, in my own words, each with an example.

Module 1: Communication, Types and Barriers
  • Communication means creating shared understanding, not just sending a message. In the Shannon–Weaver model, feedback closes the loop and noise explains where a message breaks.
  • Sync or async is a choice each time: a quick call for something urgent, an email when the reader can reply later.
  • Digital barriers are real: notification overload, tone lost in text, and multitasking. A short text can sound rude when I only meant to be brief.
  • Active listening beats judging first. I should paraphrase and ask open questions before I answer.
  • Steve Jobs sold the benefit, not the spec: “1,000 songs in your pocket” instead of “a 5GB hard drive”.
Module 2: Types of Non-Verbal Communication
  • First impressions form in seconds from non-verbal cues, and when words and body conflict, people tend to trust the body.
  • Each channel carries meaning: kinesics (gestures), oculesics (eye contact), proxemics (space), haptics (touch), paralanguage (tone and pace), chronemics (time) and appearance. Norms differ across cultures.
  • The famous “93% of communication is body language” claim is a myth. Mehrabian's finding applied only to feelings and attitudes when words and tone conflict.
  • Online, my camera framing, punctuation and reply time are my body language.
Module 3: Business Writing: Emails, Messages and Memo
  • Treat the first line as the whole message, because many readers never get further. State the point or the ask up front (BLUF).
  • Check every draft against the 7 Cs (clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete, courteous) before sending. A specific subject line is part of being clear.
  • Use the direct approach for routine requests and good news; use the indirect approach for bad news, with a buffer and reasons first.
  • Business formats have rules: emails need a clear subject and sign-off, and a memo has TO, FROM, DATE and SUBJECT but no greeting.

My professional vocabulary

Words and phrases from class that I now use.

  1. Noise: A buzzing phone during a call is noise that can distort even a clear message.
  2. Active listening: Before I reply, I paraphrase what I heard to check that I understood it.
  3. BLUF (bottom line up front): I now open my emails with the request, so the reader sees it in the first line.
  4. Digital body language: My reply time and punctuation are part of how I come across online.
  5. Indirect approach: To deliver bad news, I start with a buffer and my reasons instead of the refusal.
